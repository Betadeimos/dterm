What the theory says, applied to D_

  • We see mass, not boxes. Software centers the bounding box; the eye centers visual weight. Asymmetric shapes (play buttons, letters) need a nudge so they look centered.
  • D_ is lopsided by nature. The D fills the full height on the left, the underscore only the bottom right, so the top-right corner is empty. The bounding box is centered, the ink is not: its center of mass sits left of and below the box center. Group A tests ways to place it by mass.
  • The optical center sits slightly above the geometric center. Things placed at the exact middle look a little low. Variants with "lift" raise it by 1 to 3% of the tile.
  • Round sides look smaller than flat sides. The D has a flat stem on the left and a round bowl on the right (the reason type uses overshoot). The left edge reads as "harder" and nearer.
  • Small first. Strokes must stay at least 1 px at 16 px, details snap to whole pixels, shapes stay simple. Group G snaps the stem and the bar to the pixel grid.
  • Windows: app icons are drawn on a 48 px grid, keep a balanced silhouette with no extremes of thick and thin, and avoid typography unless essential. For us the letters are the mark, so they have to be as solid as a shape.
